The idea

How much can one
button do?

I built an arcade around a simple constraint: every interaction has to work through one button. Four quick presses start a ten-second speed round; a longer hold opens the leaderboard.

The same switch handles play and initials entry. An OLED shows the current presses per second, five saved high scores, and a lifetime press counter. Scores and the counter survive power loss.

A custom 3D-printed enclosure brings the display, switch, microcontroller, and wiring together. The build gave me practice with embedded code, debouncing, persistent storage, and fitting electronics into a physical case.

July 14, 2026Debugging

Making display failures visible

Updated OLED initialization to use the QT connector’s I²C pins and added startup messages for bus status, display detection, and restored counter data. A recurring serial heartbeat helps confirm the sketch is running when the screen is blank.

View the diagnostics update
July 14, 2026Firmware

A complete game through one input

Added the lifetime counter, timed speed round, five saved high scores, and three-letter initials entry. Press timing separates normal counting, starting a round, and browsing the leaderboard; debouncing keeps switch chatter from becoming extra presses.
